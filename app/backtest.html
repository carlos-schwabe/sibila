How it works

Data. TSE open data for the 2022 presidential runoff: votes per section (votacao_secao_2022_BR) and section details (detalhe_votacao_secao_2022), which give each section's registered voters and the time it entered TSE's totals. The replay follows that real order. Each section's 1st-turn votes are known before the runoff and give its expected size.

Projection. For every polling place with uncounted sections, the engine picks the finest population (polling place, zone, city, state) whose counted share of expected votes and number of counted sections pass the thresholds, falling back to Brazil. Expected votes are 1st-turn votes times that population's turnout ratio. Vote share uses the population's mean share. Swing uses the section's own 1st-turn Lula share plus the population's mean change between turns.

Interval. Variance adds three parts: section-level noise, the sampling error of each population mean, and the between-group variance of every level the projection skips (a city projected from its state carries the city-around-state variance, and so on). Counting order is not random, so a share ρ of that skipped variance is treated as one shared bias per population.
